Master’s Thesis – Structural Preliminary Design of Propeller Blades for Turboprop Engines

Overview

Title
Development and Verification of a Structural Conceptual Design Method for Propeller Blades
Institution
Technical University of Munich (TUM)
Company
MTU Aero Engines AG
Department
Preliminary Design of Civil and Military Programs
Completion
March 2026

Objective

Development and verification of a multidisciplinary preliminary design tool for propeller blades, intended for use in the conceptual design phase of propeller-driven aero engines. The tool enables the rapid assessment and optimization of propeller configurations and supports MTU’s preliminary design team in the context of both conventional turboprop engines and novel concepts such as Open Rotor and Propfan architectures.

Methodology and Implementation

  • Coupling of aerodynamic blade geometry optimization (maximizing propeller efficiency) with an iterative structural sizing procedure aimed at weight minimization while ensuring structural integrity (Figure 1)
  • Aerodynamic design using low-fidelity methods (BEMT - Blade Element Momentum Theory): computation of the external blade geometry and aerodynamic loads from mission-specific flight conditions and performance requirements
  • Static structural analysis to determine a mass-optimal internal reinforcement structure for different blade configurations (solid, hollow, spar-reinforced)
  • Full implementation in Fortran
  • Validation of the tool’s predictive accuracy against a reference propeller configuration

Results

  • Weight prediction accuracy within ±5% against the reference configuration, confirming the validity of the implemented approach
  • Structural sizing dimensions consistent with reference data within 33% deviation across all blade components (Figure 2)
Figure 1: Overview of the conceptual design methodology: aerodynamic design followed by the iterative structural sizing procedure of the blade.

Methods and Tools

  • Programming: Fortran (numerical solver), Python (post-processing)
  • Structural Mechanics: FEM simulations in ABAQUS and CalculiX & analytical calculations (1D beam theories: Euler-Bernoulli & Timoshenko)
  • Aerodynamics: Blade Element Theory (BET) - propeller design for maximum efficiency

Skills Acquired

  • Multidisciplinary design methodology for propeller blades: Complete development of a sizing tool - from concept and implementation to validation - requiring coordination across aerodynamics, structural and preliminary design disciplines
  • Aerodynamic and structural coupling in open-rotor architectures: Direct experience in the multidisciplinary coupling of low-fidelity aerodynamics (Blade Element Theory) with structural FEM sizing, in the specific context of turboprop, Open Rotor, and Propfan blade design
  • Numerical implementation in Fortran: Development of an automated design and optimization loop integrating external FEM solvers
Figure 2: Optimized skin and web thickness distributions along the normalized blade radius r/R for Nw = 1, 2, 3 compared to the reference case.

Laboratory Course – CFD Design and Cavity Effects in an Axial Compressor

Overview

Title
Cavity Effects in a Shrouded Stator
Institution
Technical University of Munich (TUM)
Course
CFD Design of Turbomachinery
Completion
March 2025

Objective

Numerical design of an axial compressor stage for aero engine applications and investigation of the aerodynamic effects of the hub cavity of a shrouded stator on stage performance.

Methodology and Implementation

  • Complete stage design: 1D meanline design, 2D cascade design and 3D CFD simulation (mesh generation with ICEM CFD, flow solution with ANSYS CFX) up to the determination of the operating line of the reference stage without cavity (ideal stator)
  • Modelling of the stator hub cavity through artificial inlet and outlet boundary conditions at the stator hub (Figure 3) – 0.7 % of the total mass flow rate is injected as leakage flow from the cavity and recirculated back into the main channel
  • Comparison of operating lines (pressure ratio, polytropic efficiency) between the ideal and cavity configurations at three operating points: design point, choke and surge
  • Quantitative loss analysis based on radial distributions of loss indicating quantities; qualitative assessment via contour plots

Results

  • The cavity generates a large vortex at the hub, which expands along the blade span and reaches its maximum near the trailing edge (Figure 4) - a consequence of the mixing between leakage and main channel flows
  • Significant performance penalties at all operating points: reduction in total pressure ratio and polytropic efficiency (Figure 6); the effect intensifies towards surge
  • Losses are concentrated in the hub-side region (< 30 % of blade span), where the stator loss coefficient is more than doubled compared to the reference case (Figure 5)
Figure 3: Structured computational mesh of the shrouded stator stage (ANSYS ICEM CFD).
Figure 4: Streamlines at hub, casing and blade surface – comparison between configurations.
Figure 5: Radial distribution of the stator loss coefficient ωstator over the normalized blade height at the design operating point (DOP).
Figure 6: Comparison of global stage performance between ideal stator and cavity configuration.

Methods and Tools

  • Mesh generation: ANSYS ICEM CFD
  • CFD solver & post-processing: ANSYS CFX
  • Turbulence model: kε model (2-equation model)

Skills Acquired

  • Axial compressors aerodynamic design: End-to-end understanding of the compressor stage design process - 1D meanline sizing, 2D cascade & 3D stage simulation
  • Structured mesh generation for turbomachinery: Experience in generating multi-block structured meshes for complex stator passage geometries
  • Internal flow aerodynamics in axial compressors: Comprehensive characterization of leakage flows and secondary flow structures via RANS CFD modelling - combining qualitative flow visualization (contour plots, streamlines) with quantitative aerodynamic parameter distributions to assess the impact on stage performance

Bachelor’s Thesis – CFD Analysis of Tip Clearance Effects in an Axial Turbine Stage

Overview

Title
CFD Analysis of Tip Clearance Variation Effects in an Axial Turbine Model
Institution
University of Genoa
Completion
July 2023

Objective

Development of a validated CFD model of the experimental axial turbine at RWTH Aachen University and investigation of the effects of rotor tip clearance on leakage losses and the associated vortical structures within the rotor passage.

Methodology and Implementation

  • Modelling of four different rotor tip clearances and generation of structured block meshes (Figure 7) with adapted wall spacing (y+ = 1) for accurate boundary layer resolution
  • RANS simulations with the Spalart–Allmaras turbulence model - selected for its robustness and acceptable computational cost for complex turbomachinery flows
  • Qualitative analysis of flow structures within the rotor passage using contour plots of relative total pressure, entropy, and relative Mach number on axial cross-sections (z/b = 0.0 … 1.0)
  • Quantitative loss assessment via circumferentially averaged distributions of relative total pressure over the blade span and mass-flow-weighted radial averaging

Results

  • Tip leakage vortices develop within the rotor passage, growing in intensity with increasing axial distance; the vortical structures are markedly stronger at larger clearances (Figure 8), driven by the higher leakage mass flow rate through the gap
  • The loss analysis (Figure 9) reveals a clear increase in total pressure losses with increasing tip clearance; the affected region expands from the blade tip towards the hub
Figure 7: Structured 3D computational mesh of the Aachen turbine (NUMECA AutoGrid5™).
Figure 8: Relative Mach number distribution on axial cross-sections of the rotor passage for four tip clearances.
Figure 9: Circumferentially averaged relative total pressure distribution over the normalized blade height at the rotor trailing edge.

Methods and Tools

  • Mesh generation, CFD solver & post-processing: NUMECA AutoGrid5™, FINE™/Turbo and CFView™
  • Turbulence model: Spalart-Allmaras (1-equation model)

Skills Acquired

  • Flow mechanics in axial turbine stages: In-depth understanding of aerodynamic loss mechanisms in turbomachinery - in particular secondary flow structures and their interaction with the main flow, along with their numerical modelling via RANS methods
  • CFD mesh generation for turbomachinery: Hands-on experience in generating structured block meshes for complex turbomachinery geometries - including control of wall spacing (y+) and mesh quality parameters (orthogonality, skewness, expansion ratio)

Laboratory Course – 2D Numerical Simulation of Airflow Around Mountain-Shaped Profiles

Overview

Title
2D Numerical Simulation of Air Flow Around Mountain-Shaped Profiles
Institution
Technical University of Munich (TUM)
Course
Computational Thermo-Fluid Dynamics (CTFD)
Completion
February 2025

Objective

Implementation of an unsteady 2D numerical solver for the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations, validated against an analytical benchmark and applied to the study of atmospheric flow over mountain-shaped obstacles. The aim is to assess whether a low-cost finite-difference (FD) approach is sufficient to capture the key fluid-dynamic phenomena (flow acceleration, boundary-layer separation, and turbulent kinetic energy growth) as a function of obstacle geometry and Reynolds number.

Methodology and Implementation

  • Mathematical model: Incompressible, isothermal Navier-Stokes equations in 2D
  • Numerical solver (Python): Structured Cartesian mesh; second-order centred finite differences for spatial discretisation; explicit Runge-Kutta 4 scheme for time integration with adaptive time-stepping based on CFL and diffusion stability constraints; pressure-velocity coupling enforced via iterative resolution of the Pressure Poisson Equation (PPE)
  • Validation - plane Poiseuille flow: Numerical simulation of pressure-driven laminar channel flow and comparison of the computed velocity profile against the parabolic analytical solution
  • Application to mountain-shaped geometries: Three obstacle profiles of increasing complexity (rectangular, smooth Gaussian, and irregular multi-peak) simulated at Re ∈ {10, 10³, 5·10³, 10⁴}

Results

  • Reynolds number dependency: Velocity magnitude contour plots (Figure 10) demonstrate the progressive transition from fully attached flow to boundary-layer separation phenomena as the Reynolds number increases
  • Geometry influence: Obstacle shape significantly affects flow separation patterns and vortex structures, as shown by the velocity magnitude contour plots for the three geometries (Figure 11) and the TKE evolution with Reynolds number (Figure 12)
Figure 10: Velocity magnitude contour plots for the irregular mountain profile at low and high Reynolds number.
Figure 11: Velocity magnitude contour plots for the three obstacle geometries at Re = 10⁴.
Figure 12: Maximum Turbulent Kinetic Energy as a function of Reynolds number for the three obstacle geometries.

Methods and Tools

  • Programming: Python (solver implementation, mesh generation, post-processing, animations)
  • Numerical methods: Finite Difference Method (FDM), Runge-Kutta 4, iterative Pressure Poisson solver, adaptive CFL/diffusion time-stepping

Skills Acquired

  • Implementation from scratch of an unsteady incompressible Navier-Stokes solver in Python, including pressure-velocity coupling and adaptive time integration
  • Validation methodology for CFD solvers against analytical benchmark solutions (plane Poiseuille flow)
  • In-depth knowledge of numerical methods for computational fluid dynamics: spatial discretisation & time integration schemes, pressure-velocity coupling strategies and stability criteria
  • Analysis of Reynolds-number-dependent flow phenomena

Semester Project – Numerical Analysis of Detonation Processes in Pipes with Hydrogen-Air Mixtures

Overview

Title
Simulative Analysis of Flame Propagation and Explosion Processes in Pipes with Hydrogen-Air Mixtures
Institution
Technical University of Munich (TUM)
Company
Everllence (MAN Energy Solutions)
Completion
May 2025

Objective

Development of a combined analytical-numerical method for the verification of detonation and Deflagration-to-Detonation Transition (DDT) conditions in pipelines containing hydrogen-air mixtures. The focus is on the influence of thermochemical parameters on flame propagation and explosion dynamics - at low computational cost (without 3D CFD).

Methodology and Implementation

  • Definition of gas mixtures: Modelling of 20 mixtures composed of hydrogen, air and combustion products (H2O, N2, O2) at four temperatures (757 K–1023 K) and four excess air ratios (λ = 1.0 … 3.88) using Cantera (Python)
  • Verification of the Chapman-Jouguet condition: Computation of the laminar flame speed via 1D flame propagation simulation and comparison with the CJ velocity to assess whether direct detonation initiation after ignition is possible
  • DDT analysis: Analytical estimation of the minimum pipe length required for a complete DDT process (Figure 13)
  • Verification of detonation self-sustainment: 0D reactor simulation in Cantera using the post-shock state to evaluate ignition delay phenomena and assess the stability of the developed detonation wave (post-shock state computed via the Shock and Detonation Toolbox in Python)

Results

  • Chapman-Jouguet conditions: The analysed thermochemical conditions do not satisfy the CJ criterion - direct detonation initiation upon ignition is not expected without additional energy input
  • 0D reactor simulation: Species time histories (Figure 14) confirm successful ignition demonstrating the physical consistency of the model. However, the computed ignition delay times, being of the same order of magnitude as the chemical reaction time, indicate that a detonation wave formed via DDT would be unable to self-sustain and would decay under the investigated conditions
Figure 13: DDT in a closed-end pipe: pre-compression waves progressively accumulate and eventually collapse into a shock wave.
Figure 14: Time evolution of the mole fractions of H2 and OH in the 0D reactor simulation.

Methods and Tools

  • Programming: Python (Cantera, Shock and Detonation Toolbox), MATLAB (post-processing)
  • Combustion modelling: 1D flame propagation simulation & 0D reactor simulation (Cantera)

Skills Acquired

  • Modelling and analysis of flame propagation and detonation processes in reactive gas mixtures
  • Knowledge of classical detonation theories (CJ, ZND) and their application to safety-related problems
  • Experience with chemical kinetics libraries (Cantera, SDT) in Python
